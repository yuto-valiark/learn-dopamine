一寸法師、地図の縮尺に怒る

The Inch-High Guide

お手本音声

A tiny man in a bowl was sailing across a puddle outside the station. Kanato recognized him from a folktale. “Are you Issun Boshi?”

“Yes,” the man called. “Please show me how to get to the castle. I have a map, but I think the scale is wrong.”

Nozomi knelt beside him. The map showed a lake, two mountains, and a field of yellow stones. From where she stood, she could see the puddle, two shoes, and a row of breadcrumbs. A pigeon on the roof was already looking down at them.

“The map was drawn by someone your size,” she said. “Let me show you a route that works for you.”

A route on Yuto's app would have sent them across a crowded square to the university library. “That won't work for an explorer who has to step over a grain of sand,” said Nozomi. She put away the phone and picked a leaf wide enough to stay flat on the surface of the puddle. She drew a simple symbol for the bridge and marked the direction on the map. “This strip of sidewalk is less than one meter wide for me,” she said. “But it represents the entire route for you.” “What's the exact place where I should mark the castle?” she asked. Issun Boshi pointed to the yellow stones next to a pattern drawn like a fountain. “It's my landmark.” “Trust me,” he said. “I know exactly how many there are. Imagine being able to spot every one from just here.”

Kanato set the leaf down neatly without making a splash so their friend could cross. Yuto held his feet still while their guest passed between the mountains. At the end of the narrow path there was a cardboard castle that children had made for a festival.

Issun Boshi looked at it and handed Nozomi the map. “Thank you. I'll give you my best treasure.”

“What is it?” asked Kanato.

“Turn the map over. The yellow stones are my lunch.”

The pigeon landed beside them. Nozomi gave Issun Boshi a whole piece of bread before the mountains had to move again.
