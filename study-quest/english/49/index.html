明日の新聞を今日印刷するには

Tomorrow's Newspaper

お手本音声

Kanato opens the library door and finds a newspaper on the floor. It is dated tomorrow. On the front page, there is a photograph of him holding a prize at the school fair. The fair is outdoors, and the sun is shining.

“I'm going to win something,” he tells Nozomi.

“Then why is there a rain cloud outside?” she asks.

The newspaper's editor arrives to pick up the paper. She looks at the sunny photo. “That's an unexpected detail,” she says. “My raincoat is already wet.” She explains that the newspaper machine sometimes prints one possible future. She could tell from its behavior that it had chosen the most dramatic photo it could find. It does not always get the details right. Once it printed an article that said a duck had been elected mayor. The duck refused to comment. “We were lucky he didn't try to take office,” she said.

The school fair is that afternoon. Yuto checks the weather forecast and sees that rain is expected. Nozomi calls the school to confirm the plan. A teacher says the fair will move into the gym if the ground is wet. The editor says she will come anyway. “Rain can make a fair unforgettable.”

The editor points to Kanato's image. “It looks as though you won.” She says that an interview with the actual winner would give her a source she can use. She pencils “check” next to the headline. “Did you win, or were you just holding the prize? One minor detail can change the truth of a story. Don't throw this away. The local media can edit it after we see what actually happens.”

By noon, the ground is wet. The fair moves inside. Kanato comes second in the quiz. He lends his chair to the winner, who is too small to see over the crowd. “Could you hold this while I climb up? I've never won a prize before,” the winner says. She hands Kanato the prize. The editor raises her camera. For a second, the scene looks just like the future picture, except that they're inside. Then the winner stands beside Kanato and they both laugh. The editor takes her photo.

The editor publishes the new photograph in the next day's edition under this headline: “School fair moves indoors after rain.” The fridge reads the article and asks why it hasn't been mentioned. It kept the drinks cold all afternoon.

“You can write to the editor,” says Nozomi.

The fridge writes, “Dear Editor, please correct your story. I was a very important public figure.”

The editor adds a short note to the article. “Volunteers served cold drinks.”

The fridge reads it twice. “I suppose that's accurate,” it says. “Could you at least print the word cold in bigger letters?”
