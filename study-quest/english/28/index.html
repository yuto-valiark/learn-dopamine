三びきのこぶたの住宅検査

Three Little Pigs and One House Inspector

お手本音声

Three little pigs ask Yuto to check their houses because he has a clipboard. Kanato measures each door. Nozomi looks closely at the walls and asks what material each architect used. One house is made of straw, one of wood, and one of bricks.

“Brick is heavier than straw,” says Kanato.

“But is a straw house as strong as a brick one?” asks Nozomi.

“Let's test it,” says a pig. He calls the wolf.

The wolf blows. The straw roof rises like a hat and lands on Yuto. Yuto writes, “Excellent air flow.”

The wooden house shakes, and smoke comes back down its chimney, but its door stays shut. The brick house, by contrast, hardly moves. Its front door is too tough for the youngest pig to push. “Why do I have the most challenging door in the whole village?” he asks, rubbing the ankle he banged trying to open it.

Their architect, a fourth pig, lets the youngest try the door on a small model. He can open the lighter door easily with one finger. He looks through it at the tiny bedroom and bathroom inside. “It's compact. Everything I want fits within this little house,” he says. “On the first plan I could only open the window.” “Will it stay shut under pressure?” asks Kanato. The wolf blows at full speed, hard enough to send the hat off Yuto's head. Yuto pulls his jacket tight. “Anyone can lose a hat,” he says. “The roof is the part we're testing. I don't mind the hat.” The fourth pig puts her hand on the model to hold it steady. This time the door holds. “That should keep you safe,” says Nozomi.

The pigs build a home together. They use bricks to protect themselves from the wind and a lighter door so everyone can get inside.

The wolf helps paint it. He removes the old lock and fits a new fastener that opens with a lever. He asks for a room with a very large fan.
