応援が静かすぎる運動会

The Silent Sports Day

お手本音声

The first activity on sports day was a race. The judge asked the audience to remain quiet. A runner named Mai was trying a new way to start. She could see the judge but could not hear the usual signal clearly. Nozomi held up a bright yellow flag so that everyone could see exactly when the race began.

運動会の最初の種目は競走。審判が観客に静かにするよう頼みます。マイが新しいスタートを試します。審判は見えてもいつもの合図が聞こえにくいので、のぞみが黄色い旗を掲げ、全員に開始が見えるようにします。

Kanato wanted to cheer. Instead of shouting, he raised both hands and waved them. Mai noticed him as she crossed the finish line. “Now I know how you cheer,” she said later, when she saw that she'd come third. “My rival in the next lane noticed, too.” Other children copied him. Soon the whole audience was moving like a field of tall grass.

応援したいかなとは叫ばず両手を振ります。マイはゴールで気づき、三着を知ってから「応援が分かった。隣のライバルも気づいたよ」。子どもがまねし、観客が草原のように揺れます。

The fridge had been asked to help at the information desk. It joined in by opening and closing its door. A cold wind moved across the table and made the announcer shiver.

案内係の冷蔵庫も扉を開け閉め。テーブルを横切る冷たい風に、放送係は身を震わせます。

“Even our drinks aren't this cold,” the announcer said after the race. He used the microphone to announce the results.

放送係は競走後に「飲み物だってこんなに冷たくない」と言い、マイクで結果を発表します。

Before the next relay, a runner asked for the yellow flag, too. “For fairness, could you let us practice once?” she said. Their old light came on automatically, but it was hard to see in the sun. The judge gave everyone a second to look up before she raised the yellow flag. Mai watched her teammates look up, one by one. Kanato drew the flag on a handout for the next tournament. “I'm glad you had the courage to ask for a practice start,” Mai said. “People in the back had a poor view the first time.” She pointed to the far lane. The judge raised the flag a little higher.

次のリレーの選手も旗を希望し「公平に一度練習してもいい？」 以前のライトは自動でも日なたで見えにくく、審判は全員が顔を上げてから旗を振ります。マイは仲間を見守り、かなとは次の大会用の配布紙に旗を描きます。「頼む勇気があってよかった。後ろは見えにくかったから」とマイが遠いレーンを示すと、旗がさらに高くなります。

On the way back from the track, Yuto kept turning around to see the team's score. “I'm getting dizzy,” he said. Two children in the crowd were calling Mai's team the Yellow Flags. Mai laughed when she heard their new nickname. Mai showed him three signs that her team used: ready, go, and rest. He asked her to repeat the last one. Then he sat down on the track.

戻る途中、得点を振り返ってばかりのユウトは目が回ります。観客の子がマイのチームを「黄色い旗」と呼び、マイは笑います。マイが「用意、進め、休め」の三つの合図を教えると、ユウトは最後だけ聞き直してトラックに座ります。

“Is that a sign, too?” asked Kanato.

「それも合図？」とかなと。

“No,” said Yuto. “That's a very clear example.”

「いや、とても分かりやすい見本」とユウト。

At the next race, the audience looked for Mai before they decided how to cheer. This time they waited until the finish to applaud, so they did not disturb the runners.

次の競走で観客は、マイの様子を見て応援を決めます。選手を邪魔しないよう、拍手はゴールまで待ちました。
