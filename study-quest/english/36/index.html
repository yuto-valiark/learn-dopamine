北風と太陽、今度は布団干し

The North Wind's Second Contest

お手本音声

The North Wind and the Sun were still arguing when Kanato found them above a field. They had invited the same traveler back for a second contest.

“He won't take off his coat unless he gets too warm,” said the Sun.

The traveler heard them. “I won't take it off at all. My pockets are full of candy. If I take it off, I'll drop everything.”

Nozomi suggested a different contest. She and Yuto brought out two damp towels. One would hang in direct sunshine with little wind. The other would hang in the shade where the North Wind could blow gently. Kanato wrote down the time and asked both contestants to start together.

The weather forecast said it would be twenty degrees with a cool breeze later, so Kanato set a timer to check both towels before the wind changed. The traveler held the basket steady. As the traveler held the basket, he took a sweet from his pocket. It had only a gram or two of sugar in it, but by now even the paper was sticky. The North Wind suddenly blew hard enough to send a towel flying. The traveler caught it. “Gently,” said Nozomi. “This isn't the coat contest anymore.” He did his laundry frequently and usually found it relaxing. It was the first time the laundry had tried to get away.

When they checked again, the towel in the shade was drier. The Sun said that the air by her towel had been almost still. They agreed to repeat the contest on another day before making a grand claim.

The traveler took a candy from his pocket. It had melted. He took off his coat to reach a second pocket.

“I win!” said the Sun.

“You ruined my lunch,” said the traveler.

The traveler said he would prefer a dry coat to a prize. The North Wind offered to dry it. “I dry my laundry in my apartment,” said the traveler, handing him the coat. “Would you like to come by on Wednesdays? My wife makes chai and I make chamomile tea. It's a good chance to sit down after the laundry's done.”
