わらしべ長者、その交換は本当に得？

The Straw and the Trade

お手本音声

At the village market, a young man asked whether he could trade a piece of straw for something useful. Kanato recognized him from an old story. The young man said he had no intention of becoming rich by sunset. He just wanted a dry place to sit while he repaired his shoe.

村の市場で若者がわら一本を役立つものに替えたいと聞きます。昔話の人だとかなと。日没までに金持ちになる気はなく、靴を直す間の乾いた席がほしいだけ。

Nozomi offered him a clean cloth in exchange for the straw. She used it to tie together two loose garden labels. The young man wiped his shoe with the cloth, then offered it to a fruit seller whose basket had a broken handle. She wrapped the cloth around the handle and traded him an orange.

のぞみが布と交換し、わらで庭の札二枚を結びます。若者は靴を拭いた布を、かごの取っ手が壊れた果物屋へ。布で取っ手を巻き、オレンジと交換。

An hour later, he offered the orange to a child who had lost his lunch. He asked for nothing in return. Yuto was keeping a record of the trades on a folded piece of paper. When he reached that line, he hesitated.

一時間後、昼食をなくした子に無償であげます。ユウトは折りたたんだ紙に取引を記録しています。その行まで来て、手が止まります。

“Should I write that you made no profit?”

「利益なしって書く？」

“Write what happened,” Nozomi said.

「起きたことを書こう」とのぞみ。

The fruit seller found them a bench. Her hobby was sewing, and she was repairing a piece of clothing with a scrap she had saved. She pulled the bench over with one hand so the young man could sit. The owner of the stall beside it had seen the child eat the orange. He was a shoemaker. “Let me see what condition that shoe is in,” he said. He repaired it while the young man helped his customers carry parcels out to the market's parking area. The newly repaired shoe finally felt warm.

果物屋は裁縫が趣味で布切れで服を直しており、片手でベンチを引き寄せます。隣の靴屋は子がオレンジを食べるのを見ていて、靴の状態を見て直してくれます。若者は客の荷を駐車場まで運びます。直った靴は暖かい。

Yuto added up the time the man had spent carrying parcels. Then he wrote “one orange” under “Value received” beside the line about the shoe. The shoemaker pointed to the parcels. “And three of those,” he said. “Carrying those goods was worth a lot to me. I depend on people who can do that. Keeping the stall open is worth as much as selling another pair of shoes.”

ユウトは荷運びの時間を足し、靴の行の「受け取った価値」に「オレンジ一個」。靴屋は荷を指し「それ三つも。とても役立った。運べる人を頼りにしてる。店を開けておく価値はもう一足売るのと同じだ」。

The young man looked at the list. “The more I notice what people need, the more I can offer,” he said. On the way out, someone from the town museum saw Yuto's list. The museum had room for only one object from the market. Yuto asked whether she was thinking of the cloth or the straw. The museum worker made her choice: the first thing in the chain of trades. They went back to find it. The straw was still holding the garden labels together. Nozomi asked how much the museum was willing to pay for it.

若者は「人の必要に気づくほど、できることが増える」と言います。帰りに博物館の人が記録を見て市場の品を一つ展示したい。布かわらか聞き、最初のわらを選びます。まだ札を結んでいて、のぞみが、博物館ならいくらで買うかと聞きます。

“Would you accept a new piece of string? It's a small thing, but it's stronger than straw.”

「新しいひもでは？ 小さいけどわらより丈夫」。

She looked at the labels. “That's a fair deal.”

札を見て「いい取引ね」。
