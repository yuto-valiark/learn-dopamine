恐竜の展示で鳥が抗議する

A Bird Objects to the Dinosaur Display

お手本音声

The largest sign in the museum's dinosaur hall said, “Every dinosaur disappeared.” A pigeon standing under the sign tapped it three times with its beak.

“Do you disagree?” asked Kanato.

The bird tapped again. Nozomi read a smaller label next to a fossil found in China. She showed the guide the two sentences about dinosaurs.

“Both signs were printed here,” he said. “The big one is older. Come and compare this feather in the rock with the shape of our pigeon's wing.” He pointed to the fossil. “You can still see the shape of an early feather in this rock. That's one way to see how bird wings evolved,” the guide said. “It's a clue to what some ancestors of modern birds looked like.” He opened a booklet called EVOLUTION. There was a photo of a kingfisher in its usual habitat. The guide pointed to the shape of a feather under its wing. “Look at this kingfisher, for instance. Its feather has that same shape.” Nozomi looked back at the fossil and then at the big sign.

Yuto asked whether a small card could explain the difference until the sign was replaced. The other members of the museum staff came to look. Nozomi held up the old sign and explained how the discovery changed its meaning. The guide agreed. Nozomi wrote, “Many groups of dinosaurs died out. One group survived as birds.” Kanato drew an arrow pointing from the sentence to a picture of a pigeon.

“So living birds belong to a group of dinosaurs. Do all species count, even the pigeon outside this museum?” asked Kanato. “They do,” said the guide. Kanato added another arrow pointing to a sparrow at the entrance.

A child asked if that meant a pigeon could roar. She sounded nervous, so the guide held the door open to show how calm the pigeon was. “Let's observe,” Yuto suggested. The pigeon looked right at him and made its usual low sound.

“Quite polite for a dinosaur,” the child said.

When the new sign went up, one line was added at the bottom: “Birds around the entrance are part of this story.” The pigeon took its usual spot underneath and tapped the wall once, as if giving its approval.
