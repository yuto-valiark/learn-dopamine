猫のじょうろで砂漠をつくる

A Rainy-Day Desert

お手本音声

At the school fair, Nozomi was making a model desert. It had sand, several stones, and three paper plants shaped like cactuses. A cat from the theater volunteered to water it while she went to lunch. When she came back, there was a deep puddle. The cat was looking into it.

“I gave it a little water,” he said.

“You gave it a little sea,” said Kanato.

They rebuilt the model using two trays. One held sand and the other held dark soil. Nozomi poured the same amount of water into each tray under the same conditions. In the sand, the surface dried first. The soil absorbed and held more water. The cat gently pulled up a seedling to see if the soil around its roots was still wet.

“I thought this was going to be a garden,” said the cat. Nozomi asked the cat to focus its attention on the graph of yearly average rainfall. A visitor who studied flowering plants put a photo of the desert next to it. “See this visitor's photo of what grew in a whole summer with very little rainfall? Even a short period of rain can affect what grows in a dry climate, but you rarely see a natural pond in this environment. The rain disappears quickly. The plants here need a quick drink when it rains, but they won't survive in a pond. Try a few drops first.” The cat tilted his cup until exactly three drops fell.

A visitor pointed to the tray. “That's much closer to what I saw in the desert in summertime,” she said. The cat added two more drops, then wrote ECO-FRIENDLY GARDEN on the tray. He used two pictures of the same spot. “Can we combine them on one card so people can compare how the plants grow when they begin to bloom?” he asked. “My first environmental project,” he said. “I can save the rest of this water for tomorrow.”

“Does global warming mean every desert is hot now?” a child asked. The cat turned the visitor's photo around so she could see the snow at the edge. Then he went back to his job: keeping track of how much water he used. He drew a careful table with two columns: water added and number of complaints.

At the end of the fair, he reported that he had used less water and received fewer complaints.
