町の時計を信じたのは誰？

Three Clocks, One Train

お手本音声

The clock on the station wall said 10:10. The clock shop next door had a clock that read 10:18. Yuto's phone said 10:14. Kanato needed to know whether they could catch the 10:16 train. They had a connection at the next station, and the boarding gates would close five minutes after they arrived.

“Which clock is accurate?” asked Nozomi. “It's annoying to have three clocks and still need to ask. I wish phone clocks would simply tell you which platform to go to.”

“The one in our window will be right twice today,” said the shopkeeper. “That's almost a guarantee.”

It took Kanato a moment to understand that the clock had stopped. “Neither of those two clocks is much help,” he said. He asked why the station clock was slow. An employee was changing its battery and had not yet set it. The main display ran on electricity from the station, so he pointed them to that instead. It said the train would depart four minutes late. Yuto started walking toward platform two. A second clock on the platform showed a later time than his phone had shown just a moment ago. Now he looked worried. Nozomi stood where she could see both boards. She read the full timetable.

“That's the westbound train,” she said. “We're going east.”

Nozomi compared the numbers on the electronic sign. “The signal problem caused a delay for the westbound service only. Ours is still on schedule. Platform one.”

They crossed the station. The official timetable gave their departure as 10:16. A woman by the clock shop was about to take the wrong stairs. When Kanato called out, she ran after them toward the open doors.

The train left just as Yuto's phone reached 10:16. As it moved, they heard a station announcement. “Could the person who changed the battery please tell us what time it is?”
