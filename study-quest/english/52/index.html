砂浜から届くビー玉の手紙

The Message in the Marble Jar

お手本音声

After a storm, Kanato found a glass jar in the sand. It contained twelve marbles and a note that said, “Count the blue ones.” He guessed that the jar had been at sea for fifty years. Nozomi pointed out that the label on the lid showed a shop that had opened the previous month.

“Perhaps the marbles are older,” Kanato said.

“Perhaps,” she replied. “What could be checked? You might be able to find the same pattern in a shop's catalog.”

They took the jar to a beach cleanup station. Yuto was helping a local group weigh the waste it had collected. An older volunteer recognized the number on the label. It belonged to a group of children who had used the jar in a school display. The storm had knocked over an outdoor table, and the jar had rolled away. The children were glad to get it back. They had been counting the blue marbles for a class game.

Kanato decided to help with the rest of the cleanup. He thought the eastern side had more plastic because their bag from that side was heavier. He soon realized he needed to count the pieces, too. Nozomi emptied a second bag into a tray. It contained more pieces, but most were light wrappers. Yuto suggested recording the mass and the number separately. “The samples should be collected along equal lengths of beach,” said Yuto. “That way, the same type of material can be compared on both sides.”

Before they left, the volunteers pinned the two totals to the display. A visitor looked surprised. “Here's something more surprising than the jar,” she said, pointing to the two totals. Together, five pieces of litter from the east side weighed more than twelve from the west. She picked up the heaviest piece. “The ocean current could have carried it here,” she said. Kanato started to write down where it had come from, then stopped. He only knew that he'd found it on this part of the coast. He added a star under the column for unknown locations. She showed him two labels for the piece from an unfamiliar shop: “Place found” and “Place of origin.” Only the first could be filled in. “That will be enough for your report on plastic pollution,” said the visitor. “Showing the two bags side by side might encourage people to notice how much they drop.”

On the way home, Yuto bought a bag of round sweets. “They're all one shape,” he said. “The results will be easy to record.”

Kanato watched him eat one before he opened his notebook. “Will that piece be counted?”

“Part of the sample was lost before measurement,” Yuto said seriously.

Nozomi wrote the sentence down. Then she divided the remaining sweets between them, including one for the next volunteer. Yuto tried to estimate the new total. At that moment, Nozomi ate hers. Their counting system was losing pieces faster than he could write them down.
