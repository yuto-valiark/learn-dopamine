アリとキリギリスの共同経営

The Ant, the Grasshopper, and the Spreadsheet

お手本音声

The ant was preparing for winter. She had stored a supply of grain and made a budget for heating her small house. The grasshopper was preparing for winter, too. He had written “play music until room gets warm” in the first row of his plan. He'd abandoned that plan once already. The other rows were still empty.

“You need to think about food,” the ant said.

“I did,” he said. “The songs express my deepest feelings about food.”

Nozomi and Kanato were visiting with Yuto. They watched the grasshopper look for something to write in the second row. Kanato asked what jobs he could actually do. He could repair a cracked instrument, carry small bundles, and remember a long list of stops when he delivered things. The ant could organize the supplies, but she had trouble fixing the draft under the door. She put a code beside each job. The grasshopper had to ask which one meant “do it ourselves.”

They tried working together. The grasshopper stopped playing long enough to cut a piece of cloth to fit the door. Then they stopped to listen. The wind no longer whistled through the gap. The little thermometer beside the seedlings began to rise. The grasshopper moved the trays back from the open window. For once, the grasshopper's favorite tune didn't annoy the ant. “We succeeded,” said the ant. “We didn't even have to turn on the heat.” The ant offered to share her grain if he delivered four small bags to neighbors who struggled to get out throughout the winter. He remembered to leave each bag by the warmest door.

There was still one empty row. Nozomi suggested writing in some time off. The ant said she could remember taking a break once, in August, while a table was being carried past her chair. It had lasted almost a minute.

That evening, the grasshopper played an upbeat tune while everyone ate. For once, the ant did not count the grains on her plate. She counted the notes instead.

“Is that work?” Kanato asked.

“A little,” she said, putting down her pencil. Then she asked whether they could do the whole thing again on Sunday. The grasshopper carefully wrote “Nothing urgent” in the spare row.
