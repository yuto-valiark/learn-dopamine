Rapunzel and the Lift

お手本音声

Rapunzel was tired of people pulling her hair whenever they wanted to visit. She built a small lift in her tower. It could carry a basket of bread, one cat, or a visitor who was willing to stand very still. On its first day, the lift stopped halfway up.

A prince riding past saw a rope outside. He was a beginner at climbing, so he gave it a gentle tug before he put his weight on it. “Is this the hair that I'm supposed to climb?”

“No,” said Rapunzel. “That's the rope we use to ring the breakfast bell.”

He pulled it. A baker appeared at the entrance, carrying a tray of rolls.

Kanato inspected the lift with Rapunzel. The rope that they had used was too thick for one wheel. Yuto found a thinner rope in the baker's cart. Nozomi asked a girl who used a wheelchair to try reaching the call button. The girl could reach the lift, but the button was too high.

“I thought a tall tower needed tall buttons,” Rapunzel said, trying to sound as if it had been her idea. The girl looked upset until Rapunzel took the old button down.

“Only for very tall fingers,” said the girl.

Rapunzel handed the girl the wooden board with her new design. “I wanted the button to use a visual signal, so it wouldn't depend on sound,” she said. “See how it lights up when the lift is on its way?” “Show me where you can reach without leaning,” she said. “I'll put the call button here,” said the girl, marking a spot she could reach. Then she marked that height on the inside wall for the emergency alarm. “Now I can call for assistance even if the lift stops again.”

They lowered the call button and the alarm and made the entrance wider. The first passenger was the girl. She shared her roll with the cat as the lift rose past a small cutout shaped like a star. Below, the prince was still trying to get the baker to take his breakfast order.
