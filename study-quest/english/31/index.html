The Library That Was Too Quiet

お手本音声

When Kanato and Nozomi visited the town library, seven people were standing around an empty chair. A sign on the wall explained that a guest reader was expected. The librarian had promised a short folk tale about a witch and a dragon. The room fell into silence. Yuto finally asked a librarian where the guest was. She pointed at the chair.

“He's getting ready,” she whispered.

A man in the next room sneezed. A smile spread across the front row. Everyone clapped.

“That sounded like a sound effect from a horror film,” whispered Kanato. Nozomi pointed to a poster on display. The guest was reading an adaptation of a fantasy tale about a town where nobody made a sound. The witch was wise but had a terrible sense of humor. Yuto tried to copy the man's stern facial expression from the magazine, which had called the story a comedy. “I can't guess the genre from his face,” he whispered. That only made Kanato laugh.

The books had been arranged by subject, so Nozomi searched the fiction section. Her brother suggested checking the high shelf. There was a book with a bell on its cover and the author's name below it and a label that read RESERVED FOR THE AUTHOR.

Nozomi took the book to the empty chair. Meanwhile, a man hurried out from the next room, putting on a pair of glasses with one hand and holding a tissue in the other.

“That's my book,” he said. “I thought I'd lost it. Thank you. I'm shocked you heard me through the wall. Shall we start?”

A child raised her hand. “Was that sneeze part of the adventure, or did you try it just for us?” She tried to mimic the sound and put so much emotion into it that the people in the back jumped. “You could be on stage,” said Yuto. “You certainly know how to impress an audience.” The man reached for his tissue again.

“It is now,” said the reader. He opened his book. “Once upon a time, the whole town waited for someone to say bless you.”
