The Ghost Who Lost Count

お手本音声

The town museum asked for volunteers to pack old kitchen objects before repairs began. By the time Nozomi and Kanato arrived, the staff had already moved the plates into ten identical boxes. A white sticker on each box showed its number. The staff had put the boxes by the stairs temporarily while they cleared a path to the truck. One label was covered with dust.

A ghost sat on the stairs counting softly. “Eight, nine,” she said. Then she started from one again. Kanato recognized her from a story he had heard at school. He asked whether she was looking for a missing plate.

“No,” said the ghost. “I've found all the plates I lost in that story. I'm trying to count the ones you packed.”

Yuto checked the records. Someone had written “ten plates” before the boxes had been closed, but nobody had confirmed that there was a plate in each box. Nozomi opened the one with the dusty label. Instead of a plate, they found a paper hat from a museum workshop. Kanato put the hat on and twisted it around to see whether it had a plate hidden inside.

“Maybe the plate broke,” said Kanato.

The ghost looked at the inventory on Yuto's clipboard. The director had written a note in the margin: “If an item is missing, look where it was used. Don't assume it has been thrown in the trash.” The ghost pointed to the doorway with her mop. “People rush when they clean. I do it myself.” “It worked for the missing go-cart. We found it outside, where the children had been riding.” “Does it say anything about damage to this item?” Yuto shook his head. He held up a photograph clipped to the last page.

In the photograph, an ordinary plate was sitting under a plant. A small coaster sat between the plate and the plant pot to keep the pot level. They went back to the old room. The plant had already been moved. By bending down, Nozomi noticed the shape of the dish behind a curtain, where a cleaning cloth had been hung up to dry. To reach it she had to move the pole that held the curtain up. The ghost caught the curtain before it could fall onto the plate. Nozomi recorded the plate's condition and put it into the empty box.

“Ten,” said the ghost at last. Everyone expected her to disappear. She followed them down the street instead. The talking fridge was waiting at home with a chair and a plate of snacks.

“How many shelves have you got?” the ghost asked.

“Three,” said the fridge.

“Are you counting the door?”

The fridge looked worried. It had never counted the door, though it knew exactly how many cookies were on each shelf.
